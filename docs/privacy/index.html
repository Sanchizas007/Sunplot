Privacy

Sunplot has no account and no analytics. Your location, your spots and the skylines you trace never leave your device; the only data it sends is a purchase record and an anonymous Apple Ads code, both explained below.

What stays on the device

  • Location. Used to work out where the sun travels overhead, because the answer changes with latitude. It is read on the device and never transmitted.
  • The camera. The live view lets you trace the skyline. No photo or video is recorded, saved or sent.
  • Motion. The compass tells the app which way the phone points, so the sun's path lines up with what the camera sees.
  • Your spots. The places you save and the skylines you trace are stored in the app's own storage on the device.

What we collect

Nothing about your spots and nothing about how you use the app: no account, no usage analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising in the app. The one third party in the app is RevenueCat, which receives the purchase and ad-campaign data described below. The App Store privacy label lists exactly those two kinds of data, purchases and advertising data, neither linked to you and neither used for tracking.

Purchases and RevenueCat

The unlock is a one-time purchase through the App Store, and your phone decides whether you have it: Sunplot asks Apple whether this Apple Account owns it and gets back yes or no. To see which purchases happen, the app also tells RevenueCat, a service that handles in-app purchases, about your purchase. RevenueCat receives your purchase receipt and transaction details from Apple, an anonymous app user ID that it generates, and the technical data that comes with any network request, such as your IP address, device model, system version, language and country. It never receives your location, your spots, the skylines you trace or anything you enter. We cannot tell who you are from this, and RevenueCat does not use it to track you across other companies' apps or websites. RevenueCat's own policy, at revenuecat.com/privacy, describes how it handles that data. We never see your name, card or address.

Apple Ads attribution

Whether or not you came from an ad, the app asks Apple for an attribution code, which says nothing about who you are, and passes it to RevenueCat once per installation, together with the anonymous app user ID. If you installed Sunplot after tapping one of our ads in the App Store (Apple Ads), RevenueCat uses the code to ask Apple which campaign, ad group and keyword the ad belonged to, and keeps that with the same ID, so that we can see which ads lead to purchases. If you did not come from an ad, Apple reports no campaign. Sunplot does not read your advertising identifier (IDFA) and does not show the tracking-permission prompt, because nothing is combined with data from other companies' apps or websites. RevenueCat's policy, mentioned above, covers this data too.

Children

Sunplot is not aimed at children and collects nothing that says who anybody is, at any age. The only data that leaves the phone is the purchase record and the ad code described above.

Deleting your data

Deleting the app removes everything it has stored on the phone. The one thing held elsewhere is the anonymous purchase record at RevenueCat; if you would like it erased, write to the support address and we will have it deleted.

Changes

If this policy ever changes, the date at the foot of this page changes with it.
